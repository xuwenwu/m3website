What Is Light? A 2,500 year speed run

A three minute cartoon history of how people figured out what light is, from Empedocles' "laser eyes" to the Braggs reading atomic spacings with X-rays. It sets up our discussion of diffraction: how can a wave reveal the spacing of atoms you cannot see?

The story in one line per stop

  1. ~450 BC Empedocles: the eyes send out fire. Euclid: light travels in straight lines and reflects at equal angles.
  2. ~1021 Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen): light travels from objects into the eye; the camera obscura.
  3. 1665 Grimaldi notices fringed shadow edges and names the effect diffraction.
  4. 1666 to 1704 Newton splits white light with a prism and argues for particles.
  5. 1678 Huygens: every point on a wavefront launches new wavelets.
  6. 1801 Young's double slit: bright and dark fringes, so light interferes like a wave.
  7. 1818 Fresnel's diffraction theory, Poisson's objection, and Arago's bright spot.
  8. 1865 Maxwell: light is an electromagnetic wave, c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀).
  9. 1905 Einstein: light also comes in packets, called photons.
  10. 1912 and 1913 von Laue diffracts X-rays with a crystal; the Braggs write nλ = 2d sin θ.
  11. 1924 to 1927 de Broglie: matter is wavy too; electrons diffract.

Questions to bring to class

Why does a crystal act like a grating?

What sets the spacing of the "slits," and why do X-rays (not visible light) work for atoms?

Why does Bragg's law have a 2?

Trace the two rays reflected from neighboring planes and find the extra path length.

Why was the Poisson spot so convincing?

Which prediction can a wave theory make that a particle theory cannot?
